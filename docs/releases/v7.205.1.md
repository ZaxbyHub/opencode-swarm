# redaction-surfaces-3710 unit pin re-pointed at the archived release history

## What changed

- The second test in `tests/unit/failures/redaction-surfaces-3710.test.ts` no
  longer reads the rotating `docs/releases/pending/` directory. It now pins
  the #3710-3712 redaction-hardening fragment content in the ARCHIVED release
  history: the section markers in `docs/releases/v7.204.9.md` (the
  "Redaction-family hardening" section with its three #3710/#3712 subsections
  and the `sanitizeFailureEvidenceDisplay` seam) plus the consumed-fragment
  provenance in `docs/releases/manifests/v7.204.9.json` (`fragments[].sha256`
  for `3710-3712-redaction-hardening.md`).
- Same shape as the #3509 precedent
  (`tests/unit/hooks/review-route-seams-3509.test.ts` pins its archived
  content against `docs/releases/v7.201.1.md`). Future redaction work authors
  its own fragment and adds its own NEW archived pin at authoring time.

## Why

Pending fragments are rotating artifacts: every release materializes and
deletes them. The old assertion ("at least one file under
`docs/releases/pending/` matches `/(3710|3711|3712|redaction)/i`") broke the
repo-wide `unit` gate the moment the last matching fragment was consumed —
observed live when PR #3737's merge-group CI run failed after v7.204.9
consumed `3710-3712-redaction-hardening.md`, and the pin only passed again
because the newer `3730-3736-redaction-fill-tolerance-jwt-shapes.md` fragment
(#3742) happened to match the `redaction` alternation. Issue #3745.

## Migration steps

None. Test-only change; no runtime, config, or tool surface moved.

## Known caveats

- The archived pin is version-anchored (`v7.204.9`): if the archived release
  history were ever regenerated or restructured, this test fails loudly and
  the pin must be re-pointed deliberately — that is the intended fail-closed
  behavior, not a regression.
- The fill-byte matrix test in
  `tests/unit/failures/tool-failure-classification.test.ts` ("never leaks a
  secret for any single or adjacent-pair fill-byte insertion…") exceeds the
  5 s default bun test timeout on slow hosts; it fails identically on clean
  `origin/main` (verified with the fix stashed) and is unrelated to this
  change.

# `ci(release)`: raise the cleanup wait budget to the dispatched ci.yml unit matrix's 40-minute per-shard ceiling

## What changed

- `.github/workflows/release-and-publish.yml` — the `cleanup-release-fragments` job's wait for the dispatched `ci.yml` run now polls `wait_for_dispatched_run ci.yml 480 5` (2,400 s = 40 min) under `# target-duration: 40`, up from `360 5` (30 min) under `# target-duration: 30`. The 40-min budget now numerically equals the dispatched `ci.yml` `unit` matrix job's declared `timeout-minutes: 40` per-shard ceiling, instead of sitting 10 min below it; see Known caveats for what the budget bounds and what it does not.
- The coupled job-level `timeout-minutes` rose from 70 to 80: the three declared waits now sum to 40 + 10 + 10 = 60 min (3,600 s), so `scripts/check-workflow-timeouts.ts` requires `ceil(3,600 × 1.2) + 5 min` = 4,620 s = 77 min; 80 keeps 3 min of slack. The arithmetic comment above the value was re-derived to match.
- `wait_for_dispatched_run` now rejects non-numeric `max_attempts` / `poll_seconds` arguments up front (PRR-012 from the #3242 review): previously a bad budget made `seq` fail inside the `for` list, the poll loop was skipped entirely, and the helper reported a false `Timed out` without ever polling. The guard turns that into a precise `::error::` naming the bad argument.
- New regression suite `tests/unit/scripts/check-workflow-timeouts-issue-3259.test.ts`: fixture copies prove the lint reds on the raised budget under a stale 70-min timeout (`required 4620s`) and on a 30-min budget under a 40-min target; live-file ratchets pin the new call site, the sibling waits (pr-standards/drift-check at `120 5`, target 10), the untouched `release-health-summary` job, and the cross-file ceiling coupling (budget minutes ≥ `ci.yml` `unit` `timeout-minutes`); a bash probe executes the shipped helper and asserts a non-numeric budget exits 1 with the guard message and no false `Timed out`.

## Why

Issue #3259 (#2952 residual, PRR-005 from the #3242 review): the wait budget (30 min) equaled its self-certified target but sat below the dispatched workflow's real ceiling (40 min per shard). A healthy-but-slow dispatched `ci.yml` run would exhaust the wait, `wait_for_dispatched_run` would return 1, the step would abort under `set -euo pipefail`, `cleanup-release-fragments` would fail, and the already-created cleanup PR would strand — the exact #2952 failure mode at a higher threshold (observed dispatched duration 16m05s, ~1.9× headroom today). The shipped lint could not catch it because the directive self-certifies within one file and never reads `ci.yml`'s ceilings; raising the budget to the per-shard ceiling and letting the lint enforce the coupled arithmetic closes the 10-min-threshold gap without the (separately owned, #3243) lint hardening; the whole-run critical-path residual above is disclosed in Known caveats.

## Migration steps

None. The change is confined to the release workflow's wait arithmetic and its tests; no configuration, tool, or runtime surface is affected.

## Known caveats

- The budget equals the `unit` per-shard ceiling, but `wait_for_dispatched_run` measures the whole dispatched run's wall time from dispatch, and the run's critical path is serial: runner queue time → `detect-release`/`release-owner-guard` → `quality` (declares no `timeout-minutes`; GitHub's 360-minute default applies) → `unit` (≤ 40 min) → `unit-passed` (≤ 10 min). A unit shard near its ceiling after even an ordinary quality leg can therefore exceed the 40-min budget — and queue time counts against it in full, because the `createdAt >= dispatch_after` poll already matches a run while it is `queued` and the loop keeps polling (and burning budget) until the run completes. Observed whole-run duration today is 16m05s (~2.4× headroom). This is the documented residual from #3259: closing it fully would need a `quality` job ceiling and a critical-path-sized budget, both of which touch ci.yml job definitions and are explicitly out of #3259's scope (lint hardening is owned by #3243).
- AC4 (demonstration on the next live release: cleanup `success`, health summary green) is a post-merge observation, same protocol as #2952 AC7; it cannot be executed from this PR.
